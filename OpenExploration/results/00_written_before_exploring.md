# Written before any method was tried (Sat 31 Oct 2026, 19:45)

Draft for the team to confirm or replace. Nothing below was changed after results came in.

## Draft research question
From one fixed camera, can a program tell a light grip from a firm grip by a person it has never seen,
and does it rely on the object (the sponge visibly deforms) or on the hand (the cube does not)?

## Draft hypothesis
- Expected: above guessing on the held-out member, mostly from the sponge, because a firm grip squashes it.
  On the rigid cube only the hand can change (finger posture, how much of the cube is covered,
  fingertip colour, small movements), so cube accuracy should be lower, close to guessing.
- Shown wrong if:
  1. the final method's test accuracy is inside the range guessing reaches 95% of the time
     (for 40 balanced test grips, about 35% to 65%), or
  2. cube grips are classified as well as sponge grips (then deformation is not what the method uses), or
  3. frames with no hand in them (between grips) tell the firm takes from the light takes about as well
     as the method does (then the method may be reading the take, not the grip).

## Data facts known at this point
- 8 videos: 4 people x {firm, gentle}; each video = cube clip then sponge clip, joined at a cut.
- Grips were filmed in blocks (all firm in one take, all light in another), not in random order.
